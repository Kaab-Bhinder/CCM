The Four Jobs

What is actually being automated.

Qualification

Conversational IVR/voice AI confirms intent and basic eligibility before an agent's time is spent. Sub-threshold calls filter out unbilled.

Scoring

Every call and lead gets a quality score from connected time, response signals, and historical conversion by source.

Routing

Scores plus licensing, geography, and live capacity decide the destination in milliseconds — no round-robin waste.

Follow-up

Missed calls recovered, appointments scheduled, and re-engagement sequenced — always within consent scope and quiet hours.

Boundaries

What stays human.

  • Selling. Licensed agents present plans and close — required in insurance, better everywhere.
  • Fraud judgment. Signals are automated; removals are human decisions.
  • Escalations. A confused or vulnerable caller reaches a person immediately.
  • Account management. Your campaign has a name attached, not a bot.
Plumbing

Integrations.

AI outputs land in your systems, not a silo: scores and dispositions post to your CRM and tracking platform in real time via API or webhook.

FAQ

AI automation questions.

Does AI talk to consumers?
Only in pre-qualification and follow-up, with clear disclosure, and never to sell a regulated product. The handoff to a licensed human happens before any plan discussion.
Is this compliant with TCPA and CMS rules?
Automation operates inside the same consent scope as everything else we run — see consent & TCPA practices. Marketing-material rules per vertical are covered on the industry pages.
Can we use just the scoring without the voice AI?
Yes. The four jobs are modular — many advertisers start with scoring and routing on existing call flow, then add pre-qualification.
